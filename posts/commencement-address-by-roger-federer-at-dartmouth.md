In the 2024-06-09 Commencement Address at Dartmouth College, Roger Federer shared his three “[tennis lessons](https://youtu.be/pqWUuYTcG-o?t=5m51s)”:

# 1. “Effortless” is a myth

People often described Roger’s play as “effortless,” which used to frustrate him because it overlooked the intense work behind the scenes.

> **Key takeaway:** True mastery and making something look easy require immense discipline, grit, and tireless practice **when nobody is watching**. [Belief in yourself has to be earned through hard work.](https://youtu.be/pqWUuYTcG-o?t=8m9s)

# 2. It’s only a point

Roger highlighted a surprising statistic: even though he won nearly **80%** of his career matches, he won only **54%** of the actual points he played.

> **Key takeaway:** [In tennis and in life, perfection is impossible.](https://youtu.be/pqWUuYTcG-o?t=13m15s) You are going to lose points, matches, and opportunities. What separates a champion is the ability to accept a loss, leave it behind immediately, and focus with full clarity on the very next point. [Negative energy is wasted energy.](https://youtu.be/pqWUuYTcG-o?t=14m50s)

# 3. Life is bigger than the court

A tennis court is a tiny space, but the world is vast. Even at the height of his career, Roger prioritized family, travel, culture, and service to others (such as launching his foundation for early childhood education at age 22).

> **Key takeaway:** Don’t let your sport, career, or degree become your entire world. Cultivate meaningful relationships, work with a strong team, and find unique ways to serve a mission larger than yourself.

# Bonus Technical Tip

[At the end of his speech](https://youtu.be/pqWUuYTcG-o?t=23m5s), Roger playfully pulled out a racket to give a literal tennis lesson: use an **[Eastern grip](https://www.google.com/search?q=Eastern+grip)** for the forehand, keep your knuckles slightly apart, don’t squeeze too hard. Remember: it also starts with the _footwork_ and follow-through!

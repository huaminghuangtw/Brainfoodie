“Ramen profitable” describes a startup stage where revenue is just enough to cover the founders’ basic living expenses, removing the immediate requirement for external capital.

Ramen profitability buys founders the _autonomy_ to build without investor pressure. By achieving self-sufficiency at a micro-scale, founders reduces liquidation risk, strengthens future fundraising leverage, and minimizes fundraising distractions to fully focus on product-market fit.

When you do not need to raise money, you hold the power. This results in better investor terms, faster closing times, and the ability to avoid the “desperation tax” often imposed on founders with thinning bank accounts.

This aligns with Eric Ries’s “[Lean Startup](https://theleanstartup.com/)” principles—validating the business early through revenue reduces waste and ensures the company is building something people actually want.

Remember: ramen profitability is not the destination. A startup’s goal is massive success; ramen profitability is merely a bridge across the “valley of death” to get there.
